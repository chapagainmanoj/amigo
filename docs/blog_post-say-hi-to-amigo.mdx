Productivity tools often assume that you will keep opening an app, organizing a list, and checking
it at the right time. For people who repeatedly abandon task apps, maintaining the system can
become another task.

Amigo is testing a narrower approach: capture an intention in conversation and ask for the
follow-up you want.

> Tell Amigo what you need to do. It turns the conversation into a Task and sends a Telegram
> Reminder at the time you choose, with simple Done, Skip, or Later controls.

Amigo is in pre-beta development for a future invitation-only cohort—not a finished virtual
friend or clinical product.

## The Current Loop

A participant creates a dashboard account and pairs it with Telegram. In Telegram, they can write:

> I need to send the proposal at 3 PM. Remind me then.

Amigo creates the Task and schedules the Reminder. At the requested time, Telegram presents Done,
Skip, and Later controls. The participant can also update a Task naturally, such as “done with the
proposal.” The dashboard provides a prototype view of Tasks, pending Reminders, and recent
Sessions.

This is what “reaches out first” means today: Amigo delivers a Reminder the participant explicitly
requested. It does not yet initiate autonomous morning planning, evening accountability, meal
check-ins, or general companion conversations.

## What Exists Today

- Telegram conversation and chat setup for name and timezone.
- Natural-language Task creation and status changes.
- User-scheduled Telegram Reminders.
- Done, Skip, and Later actions.
- Dashboard authentication and Telegram pairing.
- Prototype Task, Reminder, and Session views with realtime refresh.
- Limited continuity from the current Session, Task context, and a recent Session summary.
- A `/feedback` command for beta feedback.

These are repository capabilities, not proof of production readiness. We are still working through
security, lifecycle consistency, reminder reliability, observability, privacy rights, onboarding,
and end-to-end deployment gates before inviting the external cohort.

## What Is Not Shipped

Amigo does not currently have durable personal Memory, a Memory Inspector, an anti-nag governor,
adaptive coaching, Recommender/Coach/Reflect Modes, sentiment-based intervention, WhatsApp, voice,
or native mobile apps. Those are evidence-gated maps, and any one of them may end in a decision not
to build.

Amigo is also non-clinical. It does not provide therapy, diagnosis, treatment, medication advice,
emergency dispatch, or monitored crisis support. Future non-clinical reflection or Mood Entry work
would require separate safety and privacy evidence before release.

## Why an External Beta

The first question is not whether Amigo can accumulate more features. It is whether the narrow
Task-to-Reminder loop helps real people follow through without becoming annoying or burdensome.

The initial beta will measure whether participants can activate without founder rescue, whether
Reminders arrive reliably, whether Task state remains consistent, whether the interaction feels
useful, and whether anyone wants to continue after the free 30-day period.

## Open Source and Participation

Amigo's original source and documentation are available under AGPL-3.0. The open-source license
does not imply that independent deployments receive official support. Contributions use Developer
Certificate of Origin sign-off, and suspected vulnerabilities should be reported through GitHub's
private vulnerability-reporting flow rather than a public issue.

The repository is [chapagainmanoj/amigo](https://github.com/chapagainmanoj/amigo). The complete
[capability matrix](capability-matrix.md) separates shipped prototype behavior, beta experiments,
and roadmap ideas.

We will publish a trial link only after the invitation-beta release gates pass. Until then, the
most useful contribution is honest feedback on the problem, the activation journey, and the narrow
product promise.
