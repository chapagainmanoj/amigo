## Question

Does calling an experiment CBT research remove the existing non-clinical and participant-protection
boundaries, and what architecture can keep it out of the commercial experience?

## Comments

### 2026-10-03 — Research findings

No. The current wellbeing decision permits reviewed non-clinical Thought Check templates but
excludes Therapy Mode, diagnosis, clinical scales, and treatment. It also reserves clinical
functionality for a new destination with qualified governance. A separate synthetic-scenario
sandbox can examine template adherence and failure behavior without enabling a product Mode.

External guidance supports governance rather than a disclaimer-only approach. WHO describes
generative-health-AI risks and calls for stakeholder involvement and oversight. HHS OHRP explains
that human-subjects research determinations depend on the activity and applicable rules, not just
its title; the Common Rule does not automatically cover all privately funded experiments.

Sources, checked 2026-10-03:

- [WHO guidance announcement](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-01-2024-who-releases-ai-ethics-and-governance-guidance-for-large-multi-modal-models).
- [HHS OHRP: What Is Human Subjects Research?](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/education-and-outreach/online-education/human-research-protection-training/lesson-2-what-is-human-subjects-research/index.html).
- [FDA General Wellness guidance, January 2026](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices).

These findings are not a legal determination, clinical endorsement, or permission to enroll people.
